The problem we're solving

Our intended users: Irish employees aged 21–30 in their first full-time jobs, especially people managing several recurring expenses while trying to save. The user is the worker managing their budget; the potential buyer is the same person.

What they might say

“I get paid and then my direct debits hit. I don't know what I can actually save.”

“I want a simple monthly picture without connecting my bank account.”

Illustrative user statements, not research interview quotes.

Today’s workaround

People may check their bank app, add numbers in phone notes or maintain a spreadsheet. These approaches can make it hard to see all expenses alongside one savings goal.

Existing alternatives include YNAB and Revolut. Check their current features and terms before making comparisons.

Alternative website links; researcher should record verification date before submission.

Four requirements

R1 · See total recurring expenses at a glance.
R2 · Calculate how much is left after expenses.
R3 · Track progress towards a savings target.
R4 · Edit the budget on a phone and keep changes after refreshing.

How it works

  1. Enter monthly take-home pay and your savings goal.
  2. Review, add or remove recurring expenses.
  3. See the money left over and update your savings progress.

How it could be sustained

Proposed model, not a live offer: Keep the basic planner free; explore an optional €2.99/month premium tier for multi-month reports and exports. This pricing is an assumption requiring customer validation. Premium features are not included in this prototype.

Scope and limitations

This is a proof of concept, not a bank or financial adviser. It does not connect to accounts, process payments, predict returns, or sync data across devices. Local browser storage may be erased by the user. All displayed numbers are illustrative.
